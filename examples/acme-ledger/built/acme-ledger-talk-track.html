Talk track: Acme Ledger: a faster close, with a person approving every posting

Worked example · finance leadership meeting · 15-minute slot: about 6 minutes of talk, then discussion · fictional product, pilot and figures

How to use this page

Full shows everything. Script only hides cues and notes for a clean read-through. Learn blurs everything except the anchor lines (blue bar); say the blurred part from memory, then click it to check.

pause a real stop, 1–2 s slow drop the pace for the meaning softer lower volume, warmer tone click / point / move ask the room optional; raise your own hand first, wait 2 s, react to what you see

Two lenses on every segment: warmth, where you show you're on their side, and competence, where the evidence earns trust (after Vanessa Van Edwards' warmth-and-competence lens). The cues use deliberate variation in pace, volume, melody, tonality, and pause (after Vinh Giang's five vocal foundations). This page is a practical synthesis inspired by their public teaching, not their method. Use only the cues that feel like you.

Cue card

Acme Ledger takes the matching work out of the month-end close, and nothing reaches the books until our controller says yes.
  1. The problem. 8 working days per close, and most of the hours go to matching by hand.
  2. The gate. Seven steps, one approval. Nothing posts before it.
  3. The proof. 2 units, 3 closes: 3 days median, 91% matched, 0 unapproved postings.
  4. The limits. 1 line in 11 still needs a person. It proposes; we decide.
  5. The ask. 2 more units for one quarter, with a controller as sponsor.
ASK: Two more business units for one quarter, with one controller as sponsor. Then the question: which unit goes first?

Off slide 2 by 1:45 · into the ask by 5:10 · say 'pilot' and 'target', never 'guaranteed' · no prices · room moments are optional

1 · The whole idea in one line

~0:47 · cumulative ~0:47 (estimate)

Purpose: Give the room the whole idea before any detail, and make a promise the rest of the talk keeps.

WarmthStart with the week they all know: close week. Look at the people who run it.
CompetenceOne plain sentence on what it does and where a person stays in control. No adjectives.

move: step out from the laptop, eyes on the room "Thanks for the time. I'll keep this to about six minutes and leave the rest of the slot for your questions."

ask the room · optional, skip if behind the clock "Quick one first. Hands up if you've lost an evening to close week lately." pause · 2 s, look across the room "That's most of us. Good, you're the right room."

"Here's the idea in one line. slow Acme Ledger takes the matching work out of the close, and nothing reaches the books until our controller says yes."

Transition"Let me start with where the days go."

2 · The problem: most of the close is matching

~0:55 · cumulative ~1:42 (estimate)

Purpose: Make the problem concrete with one number the room can repeat: eight working days.

WarmthCredit the close team: the time study was their idea, and they did it on top of a close.
CompetenceSay where the number comes from (six closes) and call the two-thirds figure a time study, not a census.

click: the close card "Every month we close the books. We know what has to happen and when it's due."

click: the list "What eats the time is the how. Export the bank feed. Find the invoice behind every line. Chase the ones that don't fit. Post, then check it all again."

click: the number "Over the last six closes, that took us 8 working days on average. pause · let the number land And the close team's own time study says about two thirds of those hours went to matching, by hand."

Optional story, your callIf you have a short true story from a recent close (a late night, a line that took a day to find), tell it here in two sentences. Do not invent one.
Transition"So what would it take to give those hours back without giving up control of the books?"
Guardrails for this segment
  • The two-thirds figure comes from a two-week time study, not from every close. If someone pushes, say exactly that.

3 · How it works: seven steps, one gate

~1:18 · cumulative ~3:00 (estimate)

Purpose: Walk the seven steps and make the approval gate the moment they remember.

WarmthSlow down at the gate and look at the controller in the room. This is their step.
CompetenceName what each step reads and writes. Four steps change nothing, and say so plainly.

click: Read "It starts by reading the month: the bank feed, and the open invoices and receipts. click: Match Then it matches each bank line to a document, and attaches that document as evidence."

click: Explain "Lines it can't match, it explains: no invoice yet, a fee in the amount, two payments for one invoice. click: Plan Then it writes a posting plan, with every exception listed and its reason."

"Four steps in, it has changed nothing. Everything so far only reads."

click: Approve pause slow · eyes on the controller "Then it stops. Our controller on duty reads the plan and says yes or no. Until then, nothing posts."

click: Post "After the yes, it posts exactly that plan, with the approver's name on the batch. click: Report And it writes a close report that our auditors can read."

Transition"That's the design. Here's what happened when we tried it."

4 · The pilot: eight days down to three

~0:57 · cumulative ~3:58 (estimate)

Purpose: Prove the design with pilot results, including the safety net catching problems, not just the speed.

WarmthThank the two units by name in the room if their leads are present (names you know, not on the slide).
CompetenceGive the sample size first: two units, three closes. Say median, not average.

click: 3 days "Two business units, three month-end closes. The median close went from eight working days to three."

click: 91% "In one October close, it read 12,412 bank lines and matched 91 percent of them, each with its document attached. The other 1,117 came to the team with a reason."

click: 0 "Zero entries were posted without a named approver. Not one."

click: 14 softer · this is the reassuring part "And 14 times, the controller declined the plan at review. Each one was fixed before it posted. That's the gate doing its job."

Transition"So what does that change for the team in close week?"

5 · What changes for the team

~0:36 · cumulative ~4:33 (estimate)

Purpose: Translate the mechanics into the team's week, and land that the decisions stay with people.

WarmthTalk about the team's evenings, not about efficiency.
CompetenceEach line is a before and after the pilot actually showed. No new claims here.

click "Today someone finds the invoice behind every line. With Acme Ledger they review the lines it couldn't match."

click "Today we post and then check again. With it we approve a plan, and the posting matches the plan."

click "And every batch carries a name. slow Our books, our approval, our record."

Transition"Before I ask for anything, here's what it doesn't do."

6 · What it doesn't do

~0:36 · cumulative ~5:09 (estimate)

Purpose: Earn trust by naming the limits before anyone else does.

WarmthSay the limits in the same calm voice as the results. No apology.
CompetenceEach limit is something the pilot showed or did not test. Say which.

click "It doesn't decide for us. It proposes a plan, and a person approves or declines it."

click "It doesn't clear every line. About one line in eleven still needs a person, and it says why for each one."

click "And it doesn't replace the audit. It writes the evidence; our auditors still read it."

Transition"Which brings me to what I'm asking for."

7 · The ask, then questions

~0:42 · cumulative ~5:50 (estimate)

Purpose: Tie the ask to the calendar, make it small enough to say yes to today, then open the room.

WarmthStep toward the room for the ask. Then stop talking.
CompetenceThe ask has a size, a length and a named role. The target is labelled a target.

click: 10 weeks "The year-end close starts in ten weeks. That's the one we most want to be calm."

click: the ask move: toward the room pause "So here's the ask. Two more business units, for one quarter, with one controller as sponsor. Our target is a close of four working days or fewer in those units."

click: the question "Thank you. slow · an open question Which unit would you put in first?" pause · then stop and let them answer

TransitionHold the silence. Let the room answer first.

Likely questions

Hard ones first. One or two sentences, then stop. ⚠ means: don't add anything, follow up in writing. If the room goes quiet: "The question I usually get is whether it can post on its own. It can't. Want me to show where the gate is?"

QuestionAnswer
Can it post anything without us?"No. Planning only reads. It posts only an approved plan, and the approver's name goes on the batch."
What if the plan is wrong?"Then the controller declines it. That happened 14 times in the pilot, and each one was fixed before anything posted."
Is three days what we'd see everywhere?"It's the pilot median across two units and three closes. The next quarter is how we find out for more units."
What will it cost?⚠ "We didn't measure cost per close in the pilot. I'll bring an estimate to the sponsor in week one."
Does it work for our bigger entities?⚠ "We haven't tested entities with more than three bank accounts. I'd put one in the next phase to find out."

The slot is 15 minutes. The talk is planned at about 6 minutes at 120 words a minute plus pauses and clicks, which leaves the rest of the slot for discussion. If the slot shrinks to 5 minutes, drop both room moments and the optional story, and keep the gate line and the ask word for word.

Rehearsal: three drills, then one timed run

  1. The 60-second version. Say the one-line idea, the 8 days, the gate line, the 3 days and the ask, nothing else. Good: a colleague can repeat the message and the ask back to you.
  2. The gate line, three times. Slide 3's Approve paragraph out loud. Good: a real two-second stop before 'Then it stops', a slower pace after it, and your eyes on one person.
  3. The hardest question, cold. Have someone ask 'What will it cost?' without warning. Good: the ⚠ answer in under 15 seconds, with no number you can't source.
  4. One timed run, standing. The whole talk with a clock and the clicks. Good: into questions by 6:00, and no line read off the slide.
