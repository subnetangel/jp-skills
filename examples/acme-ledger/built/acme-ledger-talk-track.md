# Talk track: Acme Ledger: a faster close, with a person approving every posting

Worked example · finance leadership meeting · 15-minute slot: about 6 minutes of talk, then discussion · fictional product, pilot and figures

## Cue card

**Message:** Acme Ledger takes the matching work out of the month-end close, and nothing reaches the books until our controller says yes.

- The problem. 8 working days per close, and most of the hours go to matching by hand.
- The gate. Seven steps, one approval. Nothing posts before it.
- The proof. 2 units, 3 closes: 3 days median, 91% matched, 0 unapproved postings.
- The limits. 1 line in 11 still needs a person. It proposes; we decide.
- The ask. 2 more units for one quarter, with a controller as sponsor.

**Ask:** Two more business units for one quarter, with one controller as sponsor. Then the question: which unit goes first?

_Reminders:_ Off slide 2 by 1:45 · into the ask by 5:10 · say 'pilot' and 'target', never 'guaranteed' · no prices · room moments are optional

## 1 · The whole idea in one line

_~0:47 (estimate), cumulative ~0:47 · checkpoint: off by 0:50_

- **Purpose:** Give the room the whole idea before any detail, and make a promise the rest of the talk keeps.
- **Warmth:** Start with the week they all know: close week. Look at the people who run it.
- **Competence:** One plain sentence on what it does and where a person stays in control. No adjectives.

_[move: step out from the laptop, eyes on the room]_ "Thanks for the time. I'll keep this to about six minutes and leave the rest of the slot for your questions."

_[room: optional, skip if behind the clock]_ "Quick one first. Hands up if you've lost an evening to close week lately." _[pause: 2 s, look across the room]_ "That's most of us. Good, you're the right room."

**Anchor:** "Here's the idea in one line. _[slow]_ **Acme Ledger takes the matching work out of the close, and nothing reaches the books until our controller says yes.**"

_Transition:_ "Let me start with where the days go."

## 2 · The problem: most of the close is matching

_~0:55 (estimate), cumulative ~1:42 · checkpoint: off by 1:45_

- **Purpose:** Make the problem concrete with one number the room can repeat: eight working days.
- **Warmth:** Credit the close team: the time study was their idea, and they did it on top of a close.
- **Competence:** Say where the number comes from (six closes) and call the two-thirds figure a time study, not a census.

_[click: the close card]_ "Every month we close the books. We know what has to happen and when it's due."

_[click: the list]_ "What eats the time is the how. Export the bank feed. Find the invoice behind every line. Chase the ones that don't fit. Post, then check it all again."

**Anchor:** _[click: the number]_ "Over the last six closes, that took us **8 working days** on average. _[pause: let the number land]_ And the close team's own time study says about two thirds of those hours went to matching, by hand."

> **Optional story, your call:** If you have a short true story from a recent close (a late night, a line that took a day to find), tell it here in two sentences. Do not invent one.

_Transition:_ "So what would it take to give those hours back without giving up control of the books?"

- Note: The two-thirds figure comes from a two-week time study, not from every close. If someone pushes, say exactly that.

## 3 · How it works: seven steps, one gate

_~1:18 (estimate), cumulative ~3:00 · checkpoint: off by 3:00_

- **Purpose:** Walk the seven steps and make the approval gate the moment they remember.
- **Warmth:** Slow down at the gate and look at the controller in the room. This is their step.
- **Competence:** Name what each step reads and writes. Four steps change nothing, and say so plainly.

_[click: Read]_ "It starts by reading the month: the bank feed, and the open invoices and receipts. _[click: Match]_ Then it matches each bank line to a document, and attaches that document as evidence."

_[click: Explain]_ "Lines it can't match, it explains: no invoice yet, a fee in the amount, two payments for one invoice. _[click: Plan]_ Then it writes a posting plan, with every exception listed and its reason."

"Four steps in, it has changed nothing. Everything so far only reads."

**Anchor:** _[click: Approve]_ _[pause]_ _[slow: eyes on the controller]_ **"Then it stops. Our controller on duty reads the plan and says yes or no. Until then, nothing posts."**

_[click: Post]_ "After the yes, it posts exactly that plan, with the approver's name on the batch. _[click: Report]_ And it writes a close report that our auditors can read."

_Transition:_ "That's the design. Here's what happened when we tried it."

## 4 · The pilot: eight days down to three

_~0:57 (estimate), cumulative ~3:58 · checkpoint: off by 4:00_

- **Purpose:** Prove the design with pilot results, including the safety net catching problems, not just the speed.
- **Warmth:** Thank the two units by name in the room if their leads are present (names you know, not on the slide).
- **Competence:** Give the sample size first: two units, three closes. Say median, not average.

**Anchor:** _[click: 3 days]_ "Two business units, three month-end closes. The median close went from eight working days to **three**."

_[click: 91%]_ "In one October close, it read 12,412 bank lines and matched 91 percent of them, each with its document attached. The other 1,117 came to the team with a reason."

_[click: 0]_ "Zero entries were posted without a named approver. Not one."

_[click: 14]_ _[soft: this is the reassuring part]_ "And 14 times, the controller declined the plan at review. Each one was fixed before it posted. That's the gate doing its job."

_Transition:_ "So what does that change for the team in close week?"

- Note: Fictional sample data in this worked example. In a real deck every tile matches a line in FIGURES.md.

## 5 · What changes for the team

_~0:36 (estimate), cumulative ~4:33 · checkpoint: off by 4:35_

- **Purpose:** Translate the mechanics into the team's week, and land that the decisions stay with people.
- **Warmth:** Talk about the team's evenings, not about efficiency.
- **Competence:** Each line is a before and after the pilot actually showed. No new claims here.

_[click]_ "Today someone finds the invoice behind every line. With Acme Ledger they review the lines it couldn't match."

_[click]_ "Today we post and then check again. With it we approve a plan, and the posting matches the plan."

**Anchor:** _[click]_ "And every batch carries a name. _[slow]_ **Our books, our approval, our record.**"

_Transition:_ "Before I ask for anything, here's what it doesn't do."

## 6 · What it doesn't do

_~0:36 (estimate), cumulative ~5:09 · checkpoint: off by 5:10_

- **Purpose:** Earn trust by naming the limits before anyone else does.
- **Warmth:** Say the limits in the same calm voice as the results. No apology.
- **Competence:** Each limit is something the pilot showed or did not test. Say which.

_[click]_ "It doesn't decide for us. It proposes a plan, and a person approves or declines it."

_[click]_ "It doesn't clear every line. About one line in eleven still needs a person, and it says why for each one."

_[click]_ "And it doesn't replace the audit. It writes the evidence; our auditors still read it."

_Transition:_ "Which brings me to what I'm asking for."

## 7 · The ask, then questions

_~0:42 (estimate), cumulative ~5:50 · checkpoint: into questions by 6:00_

- **Purpose:** Tie the ask to the calendar, make it small enough to say yes to today, then open the room.
- **Warmth:** Step toward the room for the ask. Then stop talking.
- **Competence:** The ask has a size, a length and a named role. The target is labelled a target.

_[click: 10 weeks]_ "The year-end close starts in ten weeks. That's the one we most want to be calm."

**Anchor:** _[click: the ask]_ _[move: toward the room]_ _[pause]_ "So here's the ask. **Two more business units, for one quarter, with one controller as sponsor.** Our target is a close of four working days or fewer in those units."

**Anchor:** _[click: the question]_ "Thank you. _[slow: an open question]_ **Which unit would you put in first?**" _[pause: then stop and let them answer]_

_Transition:_ Hold the silence. Let the room answer first.

## Likely questions

**Can it post anything without us?**

"No. Planning only reads. It posts only an approved plan, and the approver's name goes on the batch."

**What if the plan is wrong?**

"Then the controller declines it. That happened 14 times in the pilot, and each one was fixed before anything posted."

**Is three days what we'd see everywhere?**

"It's the pilot median across two units and three closes. The next quarter is how we find out for more units."

**⚠ What will it cost?**

"We didn't measure cost per close in the pilot. I'll bring an estimate to the sponsor in week one."

**⚠ Does it work for our bigger entities?**

"We haven't tested entities with more than three bank accounts. I'd put one in the next phase to find out."

## Rehearsal: three drills, then one timed run

1. **The 60-second version.** Say the one-line idea, the 8 days, the gate line, the 3 days and the ask, nothing else. Good: a colleague can repeat the message and the ask back to you.
2. **The gate line, three times.** Slide 3's Approve paragraph out loud. Good: a real two-second stop before 'Then it stops', a slower pace after it, and your eyes on one person.
3. **The hardest question, cold.** Have someone ask 'What will it cost?' without warning. Good: the ⚠ answer in under 15 seconds, with no number you can't source.
4. **One timed run, standing.** The whole talk with a clock and the clicks. Good: into questions by 6:00, and no line read off the slide.

_Total ~5:50 at 120 wpm, an estimate from spoken-word counts. Time a full run out loud._
