# Public principles and source boundaries

Sources checked on 2026-11-04. These establish what the experts teach publicly, not independent proof of every effectiveness or marketing claim. Refresh sources before making new detailed attribution claims. Read the linked content rather than inferring from a title, search excerpt, or another person's comment.

## Vanessa Van Edwards

- [Official Cues page](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/cues/): identifies Vanessa Van Edwards and presents warmth and competence across verbal, vocal, nonverbal, and visual communication. Adaptation: acknowledge the audience's concern while supporting claims with clear reasoning and evidence.
- [Author's Cues chapter excerpt](https://www.globalleadership.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/GLS22_Book-excerpt_Cues-Vanessa-Van-Edwards_Chapter-1.pdf): explains the warmth/competence lens. Use this lens to check balance and authenticity, not to label the user's personality.
- [Science of People speaking guide](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/public-speaking-tips/): includes rhetorical choices, vocal variety, pauses, movement, and recorded practice. Its “what is / what could be” sparkline is attributed to Nancy Duarte; do not credit that invention to Vanessa.

Practical choices can include audience-oriented wording, an unforced greeting, relaxed posture, and explanatory gestures. Avoid claims that a single gesture universally proves trustworthiness, deception, confidence, or hidden emotion. Cultural context, disability, neurodivergence, and individual comfort matter. Do not reproduce sweeping nonverbal-percentage or biological claims without checking original research and limitations.

## Vinh Giang

- [Official website](https://vinhgiang.com/): verifies his name and communication-coaching work.
- [Vinh's five vocal foundations post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vinhgiang_5-small-changes-to-become-a-better-communicator-activity-7331280854284939265-vQZ5): identifies speaking rate, volume, melody, tonality, and pause, emphasizing deliberate variation. Apply a few purposeful choices where the message warrants them. Do not impose constant loudness, a lower voice, or an artificial persona.
- [Vinh's record-and-review demonstration](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/vinhgiang_communicationskills-publicspeaking-activity-7259170046709489664-1qTD): recommends recording a short improvised talk and later reviewing audio, muted video, and a transcript separately to notice different habits. For a real presentation, adapt this into separate evidence-based reviews and one-change practice takes.
- [Stage Academy overview](https://vinhgiang.com/programs/stage-academy) and [official FAQ](https://vinhgiang.com/faq): cover vocal skills, storytelling, presentation structure, and building a story bank. Help the speaker collect real stories with situation, choice, outcome, and lesson. Ask for facts; never invent personal experience.

## Mark Manson

[Vinh Giang's official site](https://vinhgiang.com/) carries his endorsement. This does not imply a joint curriculum or endorsement of this skill.

- [3 Steps to Becoming Great at Anything](https://markmanson.net/3-steps-to-becoming-great-at-anything): identifies high-leverage fundamentals, consistent honest feedback and iteration. Adaptation: rank a few evidence-linked improvements and retry one change. Do not present the article's practice-hour claim as a universal validated threshold.
- [The 80/20 Principle and How It Can Change Your Life](https://markmanson.net/80-20-your-life): presents prioritization as a useful lens rather than a rigid ratio. Use it to focus rehearsal, not to guarantee 80% improvement or set arbitrary scoring weights.
