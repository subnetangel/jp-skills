# Story arc: team overview or onboarding talk (your own colleagues)

**Use for:** introducing a product, program or way of working to people who will use it, sell it or support it.
10 to 15 minutes of speaking, then questions.
**One message pattern:** "This is X, it solves a problem you already have, and here is your part in it."
**Ask pattern:** one concrete action the team can take this week (bring one case, book one session, try one thing).

Typical shape: 6 slides, 10 to 12 minutes of speaking in a 15-minute slot.

| # | Slide | Purpose | On the slide | In the talk | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why you should care | Tie the topic to work everyone in the room already has, before any detail | Title and a one-line promise in their language | Name a task they did this month that this changes; the one-line idea | 0:45 to 1:15 |
| 2 | The problem | Make the gap concrete | What the current process gives versus what it leaves to people; one number with its sample size | The number, then why it does not scale; a real example if the speaker has one | 1:30 to 2:00 |
| 3 | What it is and how it works | Walk the mechanism; make the control point memorable | Step track or loop, click-through, read-only versus changes colour-coded | One sentence per step; slow down at the gate | 2:30 to 3:00 |
| 4 | What changes for the people it serves | Translate mechanics into their week | Before / after rows, outcome language | Each row as "today ... tomorrow ..."; end on who keeps control | 1:30 to 2:00 |
| 5 | How we engage | Give the team a repeatable motion | 3 to 4 numbered stages with a moving marker | What happens at each stage, who does it, how long | 1:30 to 2:00 |
| 6 | What I need from you, then thanks | A small ask they can act on this week; open questions | The ask as 2 to 3 items; where to find you | The ask, word for word; thank them; take questions | 0:45 to 1:00 |

## What makes it land

- **Their work, not the org chart.** Lead with the task they recognise, not with who built the thing.
- **Honest depth.** Say what it does today and what it does not do yet. Colleagues forgive limits; they do not
  forgive surprises in front of their own audience.
- **A motion they can repeat.** Slide 5 should be something the team could present themselves next week.
- **Internal context is allowed, but check the share build.** If slides will be forwarded, run the sensitivity check
  with the terms that must not travel.

## Common failures

Too many slides of architecture for a non-technical audience. A vague ask ("let me know if interested"). No time left
for questions because slide 3 ran long. Jargon the newest person in the room does not know; define it once or cut it.

## Likely questions to prepare

How is this different from what we do today? What does it cost us in time? Who do I go to when it breaks? Can I show
it to my own audience yet, and what must I not say?
