# Story arc: executive briefing (a client or partner's leaders)

**Use for:** 10 to 20 minutes of your own speaking inside a longer meeting with senior people from another
organisation, often with a co-presenter before or after you, and a conversation as the real goal.
**One message pattern:** "You already do X well; we can take Y off your teams' plate, with you in control."
**Ask pattern:** time or a next meeting, not a purchase. Small, named, easy to say yes to in the room.

Typical shape: 6 slides, about 12 minutes of speaking in a 15-minute slot, then Q&A. Budget at 120 wpm and keep 10%
or more spare; executives interrupt, and a handover from a co-presenter costs 20 to 30 seconds.

| # | Slide | Purpose | On the slide | In the talk | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Handoff and the gap | Pick up from whoever spoke before you, name the gap your work closes, settle any term the room could confuse | Title, one-line promise, your name and role | One sentence linking to the previous speaker; the gap in plain words; define any two terms that sound alike | 1:00 to 1:30 |
| 2 | Their recognition | Show that the work took shape around them, with their own results | A short timeline or results strip in their terms, 3 to 5 items | Credit a named leader or team (confirm the name and whether they are in the room); the line you will call back on the last content slide | 2:30 to 3:30 |
| 3 | What it is, and why it is safe | Explain the thing in plain terms; make the control point the moment they recall | A loop or step track with the human gate highlighted; click through it | Walk the steps fast, slow down at the gate, say exactly what is read-only | 2:00 to 3:00 |
| 4 | It is real elsewhere | Show it already works for others like them, each keeping their own process | Hub and spokes or tiles, one per adopter, no other client names unless approved | One short, true story per adopter at most; say what each kept control of | 1:30 to 2:00 |
| 5 | Their next wave, then the ask | Tie the callback to what is due for them next; make the ask | One or two hero numbers about their upcoming work; the ask as 2 to 3 short items | The callback line; the hero number with its source; the ask word for word, then stop | 1:30 to 2:00 |
| 6 | Thank you and the question | Hand the room the conversation | One open question on screen; contact cards | Thank them; ask the question; hold the silence | 0:20 to 0:40 |

## What makes it land

- **Warmth first, then competence.** Slide 2 is about them, not you. Credit specific people with their permission.
- **The bridge.** Plant one line on slide 2 ("you did this by hand") and call it back on slide 5 ("every one of those
  results was done by hand"). The callback turns praise into the reason for the ask.
- **No selling when the host asked you not to.** Phrase the ask as time ("two to three hours with your platform
  team") rather than a product.
- **Hero numbers, not tables.** One or two numbers per slide, each with a line in the figures file you can name aloud.
- **Hygiene is strict here.** No internal codenames, other clients, identifiers, prices or uncommitted dates.
  Run the sensitivity check on the share build.

## Common failures

Opening with your own organisation's history. A results slide that reads as your achievement rather than theirs. The
ask buried mid-sentence. Humour that only lands with insiders (try it out loud once; keep a rescue line). Running long
on the timeline slide; put a clock checkpoint on it.

## Likely questions to prepare

What does it cost (⚠ confirm the wording with whoever owns pricing)? Who has access to what? What happens when it
gets something wrong? Who else uses it? What do you need from us, and when?
