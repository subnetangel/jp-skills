# Story arc: short pitch with an ask (3 to 5 minutes)

**Use for:** a leadership review slot, a funding or headcount request, an internal demo day, a lightning talk with
a decision at the end.
**One message pattern:** "We have a measured problem; a small, measured test will tell us if this fixes it."
**Ask pattern:** a specific, bounded commitment: people, weeks, a sponsor, a decision by a date.

Typical shape: 4 to 6 slides, 3:30 to 4:30 of speaking in a 5- or 6-minute slot. `examples/acme-talk-track-spec.json`
and `assets/deck-template.html` follow this arc (about 4:00 at 120 wpm, inside a 5:30 cap).

| # | Slide | Purpose | On the slide | In the talk | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The whole idea | Say the idea and the promise in one sentence | Title and one-line promise | Acknowledge the room's own work; the one line, slowly | 0:20 to 0:30 |
| 2 | The problem, measured | One number the room can repeat, with its sample and limits | The number, its sample size, one line of context | The number; "a starting signal, not an estimate" if that is the truth | 0:40 to 0:50 |
| 3 | The idea, and why it is safe | How it works in steps, with the control point | Step track, click-through | One sentence per step; stop at the gate | 0:50 to 1:10 |
| 4 | Proof so far | Pilot or prototype results, labelled as such | 2 to 4 count-up tiles, each with a source | Results first, then the safety net working | 0:40 to 0:50 |
| 5 | What changes (optional) | The before and after for the people affected | 2 to 3 rows | One line per row | 0:25 to 0:35 |
| 6 | The ask | A bounded ask, then a question that opens discussion | The ask as 2 to 3 items; the question | The ask word for word; the question; silence | 0:30 to 0:40 |

## What makes it land

- **Target versus result.** If there are no results yet, the slide says "target" and the talk says "test whether".
  Never draw a success chart from a goal.
- **A small ask is a fast yes.** Name its size exactly (two teams, eight weeks, one sponsor).
- **End on a question, not a summary.** "Which two teams would you put in first?" moves the room to the decision.
- **The 60-second version is the rehearsal.** If the speaker cannot give the message, the number and the ask in a
  minute, the pitch is not ready.

## Common failures

Three problems instead of one. Proof slides that present estimates as results. An ask with no size. Ending with
"any questions?" instead of the decision question. Cutting the pause after the ask.

## Likely questions to prepare

What does it cost (⚠ confirm the answer before the meeting)? What if the test fails? Why now? Why these teams? What
will you report, and when?
