## Empirical support and boundaries
- Hostetter (2011), *When do gestures communicate? A meta-analysis*, 63 samples: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21355631/ . Gestures can improve comprehension; benefits depend on what information they add. Application: assess whether gesture clarifies meaning, not count alone.
- Dargue, Sweller & Jones (2019), *When our hands help us understand*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31219263/ . Another meta-analysis examining gesture/comprehension moderators. Abstract indexing verified; full article not read in this pass.
- Fiske, Cuddy & Glick (2007), *Universal dimensions of social cognition: warmth and competence*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17188552/ and https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/universal-dimensions-of-social-cognition-warmth-and-competence/ . This concerns dimensions of social perception, NOT a validated webcam diagnostic or a proof of actual honesty/intelligence.
- Broader scholarly review of gesture: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3642279/ . Research supports gestures' communicative and cognitive roles but does not justify personality inference from a hand position.

## Integration contract for Bragi
- Each feedback card: observed evidence + timestamp + modality used + limited interpretation + one drill + cited resource + re-record comparison. If hands outside frame, say unobservable, not 'poor gestures'.
- Text can assess argument, audience fit, evidence, transitions and talk-track alignment. It cannot establish vocal melody, volume or hand movement.
- Audio can support measured pauses, pace and pitch variation, with device/noise caveats. Avoid a universal ideal pitch/speaking rate; compare the same speaker and intended section.
- Video can describe visible movements and framing; do not infer hidden emotion, sincerity, psychological condition, competence, or deception. Account for disability, culture, accent and individual style.
- Warmth/competence should be a writing/intent lens, not a numeric judgment of a person. Mark practitioner heuristics separately from published evidence.
- Useful original drill: select a 30-second segment, identify one key claim, mark emphasis and a pause, use one meaning-matched gesture, rerecord. Do not claim this specific drill has been experimentally validated.
- Link to official publicly available material; use brief original paraphrases, not copied paid course content or creator impersonation.

### Research cited by the modules

- Antonakis, Fenley and Liechti (2011), *Can charisma be taught? Tests of two interventions*, Academy of Management
  Learning & Education: https://doi.org/10.5465/amle.2010.0012 . Abstract read: managers trained in a bundle of
  charismatic leadership tactics, including metaphor, were rated more charismatic. Field study of a bundle; it does
  not isolate metaphor. Supports V2 only as "metaphor is one trainable tactic".
- McGraw and Warren (2010), *Benign violations: making immoral behavior funny*, Psychological Science:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20587696/ . Abstract read: five experiments in which violations that also seem
  benign drew amusement. Supports G4's explanation of why humour is risky; it does not show jokes help talks.
