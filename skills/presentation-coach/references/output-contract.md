# Output contract

Treat this as a positive deliverable recipe. Use the user's requested format and scope. For short excerpts, edits, or a strict response cap, compress the supporting sections and label excerpts honestly. Do not imply that a short excerpt fills a full presentation slot.

## Create a presentation or talk track

Deliver these parts in order:

1. **Brief:** Audience, main takeaway, intended action, voice, time budget, and material assumptions. Keep this to a few lines.
2. **Speaking package:** For each slide or no-slides segment, supply:
   - Purpose and a claim-led headline.
   - Minimal on-screen text, distinct from the spoken script. For a no-slides talk, omit on-screen fields.
   - A concrete visual direction, using only available evidence. Label a conceptual illustration as such. Do not draw measured-result charts from targets or estimates.
   - A conversational talk track or clearly labeled excerpt.
   - A natural transition to the next idea.
   - Sparse, optional delivery cues that explain their purpose: `[pause 2s]`, `[slow for the comparison]`, `[emphasize: decision]`, `[warmer invitation]`. Use emphasis, voice, or gesture selectively rather than choreographing every sentence.
   - Segment duration and cumulative elapsed time; keep the total at least 10% under the speaking budget.
3. **Speaker cue card:** Main message, three to five anchors, and the final ask. Keep it glanceable.
4. **Likely questions:** Usually three to five, prioritizing the skeptical audience's concerns. Give concise honest answers. Mark missing facts and proposed next steps without inventing commitments.
5. **Rehearsal plan:** Two or three practical drills tied to this script. Include an audience-recall check: can a listener repeat the main message and ask?
6. **Evidence and open items:** List material factual gaps and any sources needed before presenting. Keep citations out of spoken prose where appropriate; put them in notes or a source appendix.
7. **Checks:** The ready-to-present table from `SKILL.md` (message and ask, evidence, timing, hygiene, Q&A, rehearsal), each row PASS, FAIL or not run, with the script output that proves it. A failure is shown with its fix, not softened.

For a requested shortened fallback, write a usable shorter script, not merely “speak faster.” Keep the central evidence and ask; remove secondary examples and detail.

## Time the actual content

Use a measured comfortable rate when provided. Otherwise start with a labeled planning assumption of roughly 120–140 spoken words per minute, lowering it for dense technical material or the speaker's needs. This is a working estimate, not a scientific claim or expert rule.

Calculate: spoken words / assumed words per minute + explicit pauses + audience interaction + demos and transitions. The result must leave at least 10% of the slot spare; if it does not, cut content rather than planning a faster pace. Count only words intended to be spoken, excluding headings and cue annotations. Reserve Q&A separately if it is part of the event slot. Confirm that per-segment times and total agree. If only excerpts are requested, distinguish excerpt duration from time reserved for the full talk. Recommend an out-loud timed rehearsal as the final check.

## Review and improve existing material

1. **Verdict:** State what is working and the most consequential weakness in two or three sentences.
2. **Three priorities:** For each, cite the slide, passage, or timestamp; explain the audience impact; give the exact proposed change. Keep substantive changes to budget, ask, deadlines, or commitments visibly marked as recommendations.
3. **Revised material:** Deliver the requested scope with the speaking-package fields above. Preserve accurate content and the speaker's voice.
4. **Readiness check:** Mark each area as ready, needs work, or not assessable: audience fit; main message and ask; narrative and transitions; evidence; slide clarity; warmth and credibility; timing; delivery. Explain material concerns briefly. These are coaching judgments, not validated psychological scores.
5. **Questions and rehearsal:** Supply the compact cue card, likely questions, and targeted practice steps appropriate to the scope.

If the user requests critique only, do not rewrite the full presentation. Give concrete sample fixes for the highest-impact issues.

## Review recordings and rehearse

- **Text/transcript:** Assess structure, clarity, word choice, repetition, factual support, and estimated length. Do not claim to hear pace or see confidence.
- **Audio:** Assess audible pacing, articulation, volume variation, pauses, and tone, citing timestamps when available. Do not infer facial expressions or posture.
- **Video:** Assess visible delivery and slide use where actually inspectable. Separate observation from interpretation; describe behavior without diagnosing internal emotions.
- **Full recording:** Review audio alone, muted video, and transcript separately, then synthesize. A fresh next-day review can help if time allows; do not delay urgent work unnecessarily.
- Choose one or two changes for the next take. Compare the same passage before and after. Preserve natural variation instead of trying to eliminate every filler or movement.

## Concrete example

Input fact: “Twelve observed searches had a median of 18 minutes. The pilot goal is below 10 minutes; there are no pilot results.”

Headline: “Test whether a pilot can reduce search time.”

Visual: Two clearly labeled values: observed starting sample, 18 minutes; proposed target, under 10 minutes. Include sample size and avoid a before/after success chart.

Talk-track excerpt: “In twelve observed searches, the median time was eighteen minutes. That gives us a starting signal, not a team-wide estimate. [pause 2s] Our goal is to test whether we can get below ten minutes while keeping the answers useful.”

Transition: “Here is how we would measure that fairly.”

Cue-card anchor: “18 observed median; under 10 is a target.”
